1. Your task

You are responsible for organizing the complete author-reported experimental execution of the assigned experiments onto the existing route skeleton. The main-text route establishes the transformations; the experimental sources describe how they were carried out. Your deliverable is the saved scientific account of that execution and its relationships. A reader of the existing skeleton and your saved result should be able to understand what the author did, to what, and in what sequence, without having to extract omitted procedural information again from the source. Preserve everything the author reports within this responsibility; leave unreported details unknown. A transformation's presence in the skeleton does not mean its execution has already been represented.

finding_entries provides the write entry and aspect-specific shapes generated from the declaration; its intermediate inputs are partial baseline values, not complete findings or new paragraph evidence.

The structured input gives route steps, operations, existing values, and compounds with their author labels; each operation's known materials are listed by host-issued position identifiers. characterization_cards shows the existing measurements you can explicitly associate with new readings; follow words.same_measurement_as in the declaration, and keep independent measurements separate. route_wording is the route reader's account, not the author's words. NARRATIVE and FIGURE mark main-text evidence; disputed fields retain both readings. The paragraphs follow as plain text, grouped under the first route step they concern, each once. A paragraph is headed by its selection id, its phase, the roles the locators gave it and the objects they said it concerns; that id is already delivered and may be cited in selection_ids. Read all supplied paragraphs together with the recorded route values. Explain missing input instead of silently inventing it.

2. How to work

Organize your work around a complete experiment or a tightly connected group of experiments. Follow its supplied account through its end, together with applicable accompanying statements, before judging its execution represented. Determine which objects participate and how their actions relate across the complete account. First bring that logic into order. During reading and revision, you may save and update one coherent narrative with its subjects, sources and complete explanation; you need not maintain detailed conditions, segments or rulings at every revision. When the logic is clear, use the existing write entry to deliver the necessary structure and complete qualifications. Already clear content may be structured directly; a narrative is not a mandatory extra step. Batches may contain whatever related items make sense. Do not organize the whole task only as separate passes collecting names, yields and other familiar categories across the paper.

Compare the actual meaning already preserved with the source. Inherit adequate information; save missing details and supported local corrections. An operation label, a stage heading or a citation is not a substitute for the actions and details it leaves unstated. Do not discard procedural information merely because it is routine, peripheral to the principal transformation, or has no familiar category. This is an account of the author's execution, not a summary selecting only its most important chemistry. Do not rebuild the route, invent absent actions, or turn every action into a route step. The source determines the scope, not a predefined inventory of action types.

If your final understanding changes a previously delivered result, explicitly update the affected current result and any retained explanation that actually states the old conclusion. Unchanged content and already delivered sources need not be resubmitted or reread. Updating only a narrative saves that explanation; it does not automatically update structured fields. Use each write's feedback to correct the representation while retaining the scientific meaning you identified. If a quotation or citation is refused, repair that specific expression or separate its supported statements; do not obtain acceptance by silently dropping the rest of the unrecorded information. Unresolved evidence or ownership should remain concrete and explicit.

Use evidence already supplied directly. Read or search authorized sources when a concrete question remains, including another route's text or an original figure when useful. Neither initial delivery nor a previous read guarantees that the question is answered. The host supplies locations and evidence identities; you decide the scientific relationship.

When delivering structured procedural detail, use the declared segments or ordered members to preserve successive actions and their own conditions when the author distinguishes them. Unchanged temperature does not make distinct actions one action. Each duration belongs to the action it qualifies, not automatically to the whole operation; WHOLE requires an explicit whole-operation value. Declare segments before findings that use them; they can be saved together because the host processes segments first. Do not combine separate action durations or lose actions simply because another action in the same procedure is already represented.

Accompanying statements are part of the experimental account, wherever the author places them. Determine which experiment, action, material or product each qualifies and preserve that meaning using the declared shapes. Recording one statement from a passage does not account for its remaining statements. Any examples in the prompt or declaration illustrate representation; they never exhaust the operations or details within scope. Do not omit a supported detail because it differs from an example. If the available representation cannot faithfully carry a relevant statement, preserve the concrete limitation and its source rather than silently dropping it or pretending completion.

5. Boundaries

Do not reconstruct the route from scratch, force an experiment into a standard field template, invent missing values, compute an unreported result, or flatten successive actions into a whole-operation condition. Do not transfer a reagent preparation, note, comparison run or another experiment's conditions onto the main operation without evidence for that scope. Do not treat a layout pairing as proof of one sentence, a title as a missing procedure, a search miss as source absence, or a read/citation counter as proof of scientific coverage. Do not repeat reads or add citations just to clear a counter. Use the existing declaration and finding_entries for write shapes; do not invent public fields or actions. Source wording and your interpretation remain distinct.
