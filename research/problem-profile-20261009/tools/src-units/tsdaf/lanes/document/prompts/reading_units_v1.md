You are shown one chemistry document after cleaning: every kept block as one row, in reading order, with its handle (block_id), its type, its page, the name of the section it sits under, and its full text. Divide the document into units and answer once, as JSON: a list of units in reading order, each a range of handles (first_block_id to last_block_id, copied exactly from the rows), a kind, and -- where the kind calls for it -- the author's label for the compound the unit is about. The source_phase field of the request says whether this is a main text or a supporting-information document.

What a unit is. A run of consecutive blocks that a reader would want handed to them as one thing. A procedure is one unit from its title to its last sentence of workup and yield, even when a page break split it into two blocks -- continues_on_next is only a layout hint that the next row may continue the context. Read the actual text to decide whether the rows belong together; the hint does not establish that they are one sentence or that any source text is missing. The data that follows a procedure (Rf, mp, rotation, IR, NMR, HRMS) is its own unit of kind CHARACTERIZATION, labelled with the same compound; when the data sits inside the procedure's own block there is nothing to separate and the block is PROCEDURE. A discussion paragraph is NARRATIVE, and consecutive discussion paragraphs are one unit while they argue one point. A heading, a figure caption and a change of subject each end the unit before them and begin the next; none of the three may fall inside a unit.

The kinds are defined in the kind field of the response schema supplied with this request. Apply those definitions when assigning each unit.

The label. For PROCEDURE, CHARACTERIZATION and CRYSTALLOGRAPHY, copy the compound's label as the author prints it in the unit's title or first line -- 13, 14a, (±)-1, SI-6 -- and nothing else: not the name, not a label from the discussion. When a procedure's title names the product but the data below it names nothing, the data carries the product's label. When the author wrote one entry for two or more compounds and printed each of their labels, copy the whole printed string exactly as it stands -- `9a and 9b`, `14a, 14b and 14c` -- and do not choose one of them or drop the rest; the host reads that string as the several labels it names. When the unit is about no compound the author named -- a general procedure written for a class of substrates, a table of several structures with no entry of its own -- the label is null. For every other kind the label is null.

What is not shown. Blocks that are empty or that carry only a page number are not in the rows; their handles lie between the ones you see, and a unit whose range spans them is fine. Where the experimental part begins is not asked, because it follows from the units: it is the first GENERAL_METHODS, PROCEDURE, CHARACTERIZATION or CRYSTALLOGRAPHY unit. A main text whose experimental detail is entirely in its supplement -- the usual case for a communication -- has no such unit, and every unit is NARRATIVE, BIBLIOGRAPHY or OTHER.

Copy every handle exactly from the rows; do not compose one. No two units may overlap. The rationale is one sentence saying where the experimental part begins and what marks it, or that there is none.
