Points that are easy to lose:
- One action per substance put in. When a sentence names several substances that go into the mixture, each is an action of its own: in the order the authors give when they give one (with_previous false), and in the printed order with with_previous true on every one after the first when they give none. Only something made up beforehand and put in as one (a solution, a mixture prepared apart) is a single action.
- Counts of repetition are quantities. How many times something was done (an extraction, a wash, a cycle of evacuating and refilling, the portions an amount was added in) is a REPEAT condition of that action, however briefly it is printed (a multiplication sign, a number word). Where no mark carries the count, it is recorded with ref null and raw as printed. When an amount is printed together with its count, the amount is a quantity of the material used each time: its mark goes to that material, never to the REPEAT condition, even when the mark also takes in the count; the count is then recorded as the REPEAT condition beside it.
- A quantity keeps its kind when it is hedged. An amount, a duration or a temperature printed with a word that makes it approximate or a bound (about, approximately, ca., within, up to) is still an amount, a duration or a temperature of what it describes, with that word in its raw wording; it is not OTHER. How far a reaction went (a conversion, how much of a starting material was consumed or recovered) is a quantity of the block's outcomes.
